Violet Recipes

Photo by: Cecelia Alexander

Viola adunca

The leaves and flowers are edible and can be eaten raw or cooked. The younger leaves are tender and are better raw than the older leaves which are tougher and are better cooked.

Violet Syrup

1 c violet flowers
1 1/2 c water
1/2 c honey
Put violet flowers in a mason jar pour hot water over the flowers and allow it to steep for 30 – 60 minutes. Stir in honey, the violet tea may need to be heated for the honey to dissolve. The syrup will be blue or dark purple in color you can add lemon juice to make the syrup a lighter purple or pink color.

Violet Jelly

2 c violet flowers
3 c boiled water
2 tbs lemon juice
2 1/2 c sugar
1.75 oz package pectin
Place the violet flowers in a heat-safe container, then pour boiling water over the violet flowers. Let the flowers steep for four hours. After the flowers have steep strain out the flowers. To make the jelly add the violet tea to a saucepan along with the lemon juice. In a bowl combine the pectin and ¼ cup of the sugar. Once combined add to the saucepan. Bring to a rolling boil, stirring constantly. Add the rest of sugar and bring back to a rolling boil. Allow it to boil for a minute. Remove from the heat and pour the jelly into a container. Allow to cool in the refrigerator for 12 – 24 hours.
