Tips & Safety

  • Plant Identification -
    Always make sure you have the correct plant. Double check that the plant matches the description. If the plant has inedible look-alikes be aware of their descriptions as well.
  • Check for Pollution -
    Look at the area around the plant and make sure that the plant isn’t in an area that may be polluted. For example, plants near roadways or industrial areas.
  • Respect the Plants -
    Make sure you aren’t harming the plant while foraging. Some plants like roses thrive by being pruned. Make sure to leave enough leaves, flowers, berries or other parts of the plant behind so the plant can continue to grow.
  • Leave No Trace/Pack It In Pack It Out -
    Make sure to take any trash you brought into the area with you out when you leave. You should also pick up any trash that’s left behind by someone else. When foraging, ensure you don’t trample the plants, the one you’re foraging or the ones around it. Always ensure you walk carefully.
  • Know the Laws -
    Make sure you are allowed to forage where you are. The rules of how much you can harvest can change from park to park and season to season. So, make sure you know the specific rules for the specific area that you’re in.
  • Tools -
    Ensure that you have the tools you need when foraging. A field guide, either physical or digital, is important to ensure you forage the right plants. A breathable container for your foraged plant to ensure the plants don’t get moldy. Tools like scissors, a pocketknife, and a digging tool can help you when foraging for certain plans. For example, plants that have bulbs like lilies and camas require some sort of digging tool to get to the bulb.
